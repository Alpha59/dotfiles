# Proposed Mechanisms

## Improved Pointing Process and Pitfall Awareness

**Purpose:** The Improved Pointing Process and Pitfall Awareness procedure aims to enhance the pointing process within the team, ensuring that it is taken seriously and that potential pitfalls are addressed proactively. This practice leads to more accurate and reliable task pointing and promotes better collaboration during stand-ups.

**Steps:**


1. **Pointing Process Enhancement:**
    1. Emphasize the importance of the pointing process within the team.
    2. Ensure that team members understand the significance of accurately estimating the effort required for each task.
2. **Training and Education:**
    1. Provide training and guidance on effective pointing techniques and methodologies.
    2. Educate team members about common pitfalls to watch out for during the pointing process.
3. **Pitfall Identification and Callouts:**
    1. During the pointing sessions, encourage team members to actively identify and call out potential pitfalls in task estimation.
    2. Create an open and collaborative environment where team members can share their concerns and insights.
4. **Providing Help and Information:**
    1. Offer assistance and information when team members encounter difficulties in pointing tasks.
    2. Promote a culture of knowledge sharing and mentorship within the team.
5. **Pointing Documentation:**
    1. Maintain documentation that outlines the pointing process and provides guidance on avoiding common pitfalls.
    2. Ensure that this documentation is readily accessible to all team members.
6. **Regular Stand-up Discussions:**
    1. During daily stand-up meetings, allocate time for discussing pointing-related challenges and questions.
    2. Use this forum to provide better information on when and how help can be provided to resolve pointing issues.
7. **Feedback Loop:**
    1. Establish a feedback loop for the pointing process to continuously improve it based on lessons learned.
    2. Encourage team members to share feedback and suggestions for enhancing the process.
8. **Continuous Improvement:**
    1. Promote a culture of continuous improvement in task pointing and estimation.
    2. Recognize and celebrate improvements in pointing accuracy and process efficiency.

**Responsible Party:** Team Members, Pointing Facilitator, and Stand-up Participants

**Frequency:** The Improved Pointing Process and Pitfall Awareness procedure should be integrated into the team's daily stand-up meetings and ongoing task pointing activities.

**Note:** Taking pointing seriously and addressing potential pitfalls not only leads to better task estimation but also fosters a culture of collaboration and continuous improvement within the team.

## Story Reviews and Sprint Board Evaluation


**Purpose:** The Story Reviews and Sprint Board Evaluation procedure aims to introduce new mechanisms for reviewing tasks that deviate from expectations and includes an evaluation of the sprint board during these reviews. This practice ensures that tasks align with expectations, maintaining a high standard of work and improving overall project management.

**Steps:**

1. **Sprint Task Review Meeting:**
    1. Schedule regular Sprint Task Review Meetings as part of the team's sprint activities.
    2. Ensure that team members are aware of the meeting's purpose and importance.
2. **Reviewing Tasks:**
    1. During the Sprint Task Review Meeting, systematically review each task or story that has been completed or is in progress.
    2. Evaluate whether the completed tasks match the initial expectations, including requirements, scope, and quality standards.
3. **Deviation Identification:**
    1. If any task is found to deviate from the expectations, identify the specific aspects in which it falls short.
4. **Discussion and Clarifications:**
    1. Engage in discussions to understand the reasons behind any deviations.
    2. Seek clarifications from the team member responsible for the task to gain insights into the challenges faced.
5. **Sprint Board Evaluation:**
    1. As part of the Sprint Task Review Meeting, conduct an evaluation of the sprint board.
    2. Assess the accuracy of task assignments, progress tracking, and task status updates on the board.
6. **Action Items and Follow-Up:**
    1. Document any action items resulting from the task reviews and sprint board evaluation.
    2. Assign responsibilities for addressing deviations, making necessary corrections, and improving the sprint board's accuracy.
7. **Continuous Improvement:**
    1. Encourage a culture of continuous improvement within the team.
    2. Implement changes to processes or practices based on the insights gained from task reviews and sprint board evaluations.

**Responsible Party:** Team Members and Sprint Facilitator

**Frequency:** The Story Reviews and Sprint Board Evaluation procedure should be conducted during each sprint cycle to ensure that tasks align with expectations and to maintain an accurate sprint board.

**Note:** Regular task reviews and sprint board evaluations contribute to project transparency, effective communication, and the delivery of high-quality work.

## Local Environment Testing Before Beta Deployment

**Purpose:** The Local Environment Testing Before Beta Deployment procedure emphasizes the importance of setting up and conducting testing in local development environments before pushing changes to the Beta environment. This practice ensures that code changes are thoroughly tested, minimizing potential issues and disruptions in the Beta environment.

**Steps:**


1. **Local Environment Preparation:**
    1. Before initiating any changes or new feature development, ensure that your local development environment is set up and configured correctly.
2. **Testing in Local Environment:**
    1. Conduct comprehensive testing within your local development environment to validate the functionality, stability, and performance of code changes.
    2. Test various scenarios and use cases to identify potential issues early in the development process.
3. **Local Lambda Testing Review Meeting:**
    1. Schedule and participate in a Local Lambda Testing Review Meeting with the team.
    2. During the meeting, share insights and findings from testing in your local environment.
    3. Discuss any challenges, bugs, or improvements related to the code changes.
4. **Feedback and Collaboration:**
    1. Encourage open communication and collaboration within the team during the review meeting.
    2. Seek input and feedback from team members regarding your local environment testing results.
5. **Action Items and Resolutions:**
    1. Document any action items, bug reports, or resolutions identified during the review meeting.
    2. Assign responsibilities for addressing identified issues and improving code quality.
6. **Beta Deployment Consideration:**
    1. Only proceed with pushing changes to the Beta environment once local environment testing has been successfully completed and any identified issues have been addressed.

**Responsible Party:** Development Team Member

**Frequency:** The Local Environment Testing Before Beta Deployment procedure should be followed for each code change or new feature development to ensure thorough testing in local environments.

**Note:** Local environment testing is a critical step in maintaining code quality, reducing errors, and ensuring a smoother deployment process in the Beta environment. Collaboration within the team during the Local Lambda Testing Review Meeting enhances knowledge sharing and quality assurance.

## Comprehensive Story Documentation

**Purpose:** The Comprehensive Story Documentation procedure outlines guidelines for including essential details and context in user stories. This ensures that stories are well-documented, coherent, and valuable for both team members picking up the work and the pointing process.

**Steps:**


1. **Initial Story Review:** During the story creation process, the story creator should review the user story for any potential gaps in information or context.
2. **Research Identification:** If the user story requires additional research or investigation to clarify specific aspects, clearly identify these areas within the story. Use comments, annotations, or designated sections for this purpose.
3. **Context Inclusion:** Ensure that the user story provides sufficient context for team members to understand the scope, objectives, and expected outcomes. Include relevant information such as user personas, acceptance criteria, and dependencies.
4. **Pointing Consideration:** Recognize that user story documentation is not solely for the person picking up the work but also for pointing during sprint planning. When additional context or details are extracted from the original context, update the story to include these details.
5. **Story Bar Raising:** Embrace the practice of Story Bar Raising to ensure that tickets in the Sprint Input queue are coherent and contain enough information to initiate work. Collaborate with the Story Bar Raiser (SBR) to address any deficiencies in story documentation.
6. **Anticipate Potential Problems:** While writing user stories, anticipate potential challenges or questions that team members may encounter during implementation. Include preemptive information or considerations to facilitate problem-solving.
7. **Feedback Loop:** Establish a feedback loop with team members and stakeholders to gather input on the completeness and clarity of user stories. Encourage feedback that contributes to improved story documentation.
8. **Documentation Standardization:** Ensure that user story documentation follows a standardized format or template that includes sections for essential information, research needs, and context.
9. **Documentation Training:** Provide training or guidance to team members on the importance of comprehensive story documentation and the procedures for including necessary details.
10. **Continuous Improvement:** Continuously refine the process of story documentation based on feedback and lessons learned from previous sprints. Adapt the procedure to address evolving needs.

**Responsible Party:** Story Creator, Story Bar Raiser (SBR), and Team Members

**Frequency:** This procedure should be followed for every user story created or refined during sprint planning and backlog grooming.

**Note:** The procedure aims to enhance the quality and completeness of user story documentation, ensuring that stories are valuable assets for both the team and the pointing process.

## Consistent Sprint Punting Practices

**Purpose:** The Consistent Sprint Punting Practices procedure establishes a standardized approach to the practice of "punting" tasks during sprint planning meetings and when adding new tasks mid-sprint. This ensures that punting is performed consistently and transparently across all sprint-related activities.

**Steps:**


1. **Definition of Punting:** Clearly define what constitutes "punting" within the context of sprint planning and task management. Ensure that team members have a shared understanding of this practice.
2. **Sprint Planning Meeting:** During sprint planning meetings, designate a specific time for discussing and evaluating tasks that may need to be punted. Encourage team members to raise concerns or propose punting when necessary.
3. **Punting Criteria:** Establish specific criteria or guidelines for when a task can be considered for punting. Criteria may include issues related to complexity, uncertainty, or dependencies.
4. **Punting Assessment:** Evaluate tasks against the established criteria to determine if punting is warranted. Engage the team in discussions to reach a consensus on whether a task should be punted or not.
5. **Documentation:** Clearly document the reasons for punting a task, including the identified challenges or uncertainties. Ensure that this information is easily accessible to the team.
6. **Reassessment:** Periodically reassess punted tasks to determine if they can be reintroduced into the current sprint or if the concerns have been addressed. Maintain transparency regarding the status of punted tasks.
7. **Mid-Sprint Additions:** When new tasks are added mid-sprint, apply the same punting criteria and evaluation process. Ensure that the team discusses the impact of these additions on the sprint's goals and capacity.
8. **Capacity Consideration:** Regularly review the team's capacity and workload to prevent overburdening during sprint planning or when adding new tasks. Avoid exceeding the team's sustainable velocity.
9. **Retrospective Reflection:** Include a discussion on punting practices during sprint retrospectives. Solicit feedback from team members to continuously improve the consistency and effectiveness of punting.
10. **Continuous Improvement:** Adapt and refine the punting criteria and practices based on lessons learned and evolving project needs. Ensure that the team remains flexible while adhering to consistent practices.

**Responsible Party:** Scrum Master or Sprint Planning Facilitator

**Frequency:** The procedure should be applied during each sprint planning meeting and when new tasks are introduced mid-sprint.

**Note:** This procedure aims to maintain transparency and consistency in punting practices to optimize sprint planning and task management.

## Collaborative Away Team Agreements

**Purpose:** The Collaborative Away Team Agreements procedure outlines the steps for negotiating, establishing, and maintaining collaborative support agreements for future "away team" engagements. The goal is to prevent difficulties encountered in previous agreements, such as those with Evolve and Incorrect Warranty.

**Steps:**


1. **Agreement Preparation:** Prior to engaging in an "away team" agreement, thoroughly prepare by defining the scope, objectives, and expectations of the collaboration. Document these details for reference.
2. **Cross-Team Discussion:** Initiate discussions with the involved teams to explore their needs, challenges, and objectives related to the agreement. Encourage open communication to identify potential areas of concern.
3. **Documented Agreements:** Create detailed and documented agreements that outline the responsibilities, timelines, deliverables, and dependencies of each party involved. Ensure that all terms are clear and well-understood.
4. **Collaborative Support:** Establish a framework for collaborative support during the agreement. Define the channels and methods of communication, including regular check-ins, status updates, and issue resolution processes.
5. **Designated Contacts:** Assign designated points of contact or liaisons from each team involved in the agreement. These contacts will facilitate communication and issue resolution throughout the engagement.
6. **Dependency Tracking:** Implement a system for tracking and managing dependencies between teams. Regularly review and update these dependencies to ensure alignment with project goals.
7. **Regular Progress Reporting:** Schedule and conduct regular progress reporting meetings or updates to assess the status of the agreement. Discuss any challenges, changes, or adjustments required.
8. **Issue Resolution:** Define a clear process for identifying, escalating, and resolving issues that may arise during the agreement. Ensure that responsible parties are designated for issue resolution.
9. **Documentation Repository:** Maintain a centralized repository for all documentation related to the agreement, including meeting notes, correspondence, and updated terms.
10. **Post-Agreement Review:** After the completion of the "away team" engagement, conduct a comprehensive review to evaluate the success of the agreement, identify lessons learned, and document improvements for future agreements.

**Responsible Party:** Agreement Coordinator or Team Lead

**Frequency:** The procedure should be followed for each "away team" agreement or collaborative engagement, from negotiation to post-agreement review.

**Note:** The procedure aims to enhance the negotiation and execution of collaborative away team agreements to prevent difficulties encountered in previous engagements.

## Collaborating with Other Teams Efficiently


**Purpose:** The Collaborating with Other Teams Efficiently procedure outlines the steps for establishing a systematic approach to initiate communication and collaboration with other teams in a timely manner, promoting effective cross-team coordination and knowledge sharing.

**Steps:**


1. **Team Liaison Designation:** Identify team members or roles responsible for acting as liaisons between your team and other teams. These individuals will facilitate communication and collaboration efforts.
2. **Inter-Team Communication Channels:** Establish clear communication channels and platforms for inter-team communication. Determine which tools, such as messaging apps, email, or collaborative project management platforms, are most suitable.
3. **Early Notification:** Encourage team members to proactively share information or raise concerns that may require collaboration with other teams. Promptly notify the designated liaisons about any potential inter-team dependencies.
4. **Liaison Responsibilities:** Clearly define the responsibilities of team liaisons, including monitoring communication channels for inter-team discussions, attending cross-team meetings, and facilitating information exchange.
5. **Cross-Team Meetings:** Schedule regular cross-team meetings or sync-ups to discuss ongoing projects, dependencies, and shared goals. Ensure that these meetings have a clear agenda and involve relevant stakeholders.
6. **Information Sharing:** Implement a process for sharing relevant documentation, project updates, or key information with other teams. Use a centralized repository or shared documentation platform for easy access.
7. **Dependencies Tracking:** Maintain a record of inter-team dependencies and track their progress. Ensure that dependencies are regularly reviewed and addressed to prevent delays.
8. **Escalation Process:** Establish an escalation process for cases where inter-team issues or dependencies cannot be resolved at the team liaison level. Define responsible parties for escalating and resolving such issues.
9. **Feedback Mechanism:** Encourage team members to provide feedback on the effectiveness of inter-team collaboration efforts. Use feedback to refine the procedure and improve collaboration.
10. **Training and Awareness:** Conduct training or awareness sessions for team members to promote the importance of early inter-team communication and collaboration.

**Responsible Party:** Team Lead or Collaboration Coordinator

**Frequency:** The procedure should be followed whenever there is a need for inter-team communication or collaboration, with regular cross-team meetings as determined.

**Note:** The procedure aims to enhance cross-team coordination and communication to ensure that inter-team dependencies are addressed promptly and efficiently.

## Enforcing Operational Excellence (OE) Tasks


**Purpose:** The Enforcing Operational Excellence (OE) Tasks procedure outlines the process for potentially enforcing OE tasks for developers in future sprints, ensuring that operational excellence remains a priority within the team's workflow.

**Steps:**


1. **OE Task Identification:** Identify specific OE tasks that may be enforced for developers in future sprints. These tasks should align with the team's operational excellence goals and priorities.
2. **Sprint Planning:** During sprint planning sessions, assess the feasibility of incorporating OE tasks into upcoming sprints. Consider the team's capacity and the impact of these tasks on sprint goals.
3. **Task Allocation:** If OE tasks are deemed essential, allocate them to developers within the sprint backlog. Ensure that developers are aware of their responsibilities regarding these tasks.
4. **Documentation:** Maintain clear documentation of the OE tasks assigned to developers, including task descriptions, deadlines, and expected outcomes.
5. **Monitoring and Reporting:** Continuously monitor the progress of OE tasks throughout the sprint. Use reporting and tracking mechanisms to assess task completion and identify any challenges.
6. **Feedback and Adjustments:** Encourage developers to provide feedback on the impact of OE tasks on their workload and sprint goals. Be open to making adjustments as necessary to maintain a balanced workload.
7. **Documentation Review:** At the end of each sprint, review the documentation related to enforced OE tasks. Evaluate the outcomes and their contribution to operational excellence.
8. **Communication:** Maintain open and transparent communication with the development team regarding the importance of OE tasks and their role in achieving overall team and organizational objectives.
9. **Continuous Improvement:** Use feedback and retrospective insights to continuously improve the process of enforcing OE tasks in future sprints.

**Responsible Party:** Sprint Lead or Scrum Master

**Frequency:** OE task enforcement should be considered during each sprint planning session, with ongoing monitoring and documentation review throughout the sprints.

**Note:** The actual enforcement of OE tasks may vary based on team dynamics and specific sprint goals.

## Respectful Disagree and Commit

Approaching an argument with good faith is essential for fostering constructive and meaningful discussions. Here's how you can do it:


1. **Listen Attentively:** Pay close attention to the other person's perspective, without immediately formulating counterarguments in your mind.
2. **Assume Honesty:** Give the other person the benefit of the doubt. Believe that they are expressing their genuine beliefs and feelings, even if you disagree.
3. **Stay Calm and Respectful:** Keep the tone civil and respectful. Avoid personal attacks, insults, passive-agressiveness, or condescension.
4. **Ask Questions:** Seek clarity by asking open-ended questions about their viewpoint.
5. **Find Common Ground:** Identify areas of agreement or shared concerns. This sets a positive tone for the conversation and can lead to more productive exchanges.
6. **Be Open to Changing Your Mind:** Be willing to adjust your stance if the other person presents compelling evidence or reasoning.
7. **Avoid Trolling or Provoking:** Don't use inflammatory language or tactics to provoke the other person. Focus on the merits rather than trying to "win" the conversation.
8. **Know When to Disengage:** If the discussion becomes unproductive, repetitive, or hostile, it's okay to disengage respectfully.

Engaging in good faith discussions doesn't necessarily mean you have to agree with the other person. It means you're committed to a respectful and open exchange of ideas that promotes understanding and learning, even in the face of disagreement. Avoiding logical fallacies requires careful thinking and communication. Here's how to steer clear of them:


1. **Educate Yourself:** Familiarize yourself with common logical fallacies, so you can recognize them when they occur.
2. **Engage in Self-Reflection:** Regularly evaluate your arguments for any signs of fallacies. Be honest with yourself about your reasoning.
3. **Ask for Clarification:** If someone's argument seems unclear or ambiguous, ask them to clarify their points before responding.
4. **Be Open to Change:** Be willing to change your mind if presented with convincing evidence, even if it means letting go of an argument you previously supported.
5. **Use Counterexamples:** Test your arguments by trying to think of counterexamples that could disprove your point.
6. **Break Down Complex Issues:** Some fallacies thrive in complex, convoluted discussions. Break down the topic to simpler components to prevent fallacious reasoning.
7. **Don't Oversimplify:** Avoid reducing complex issues to black-and-white or overly simplistic terms.
8. **Consider Intent:** Be mindful of whether you're genuinely trying to reach the truth, or if you're more focused on "winning" the argument.

Avoiding logical fallacies takes practice and self-awareness.

## Don’t Appeal to Ignorance

Over the past 4 years, the team has seen many changes. One thing that has not changed, is the use of the "Appeal to Ignorance" argument as a way to disagree without reasoning. This methodology has been utilized as a way to prevent projects moving forward, prevent mechanism changes, or prevent good faith discussions. SDEs, TPMs, and SDMs have used this argument as a way to prevent the opinions of others moving forward.

The "Appeal to Ignorance" is a logical fallacy that occurs when someone argues that a proposition must be false simply because it hasn't been proven true or vice versa. In the context of team discussion, this fallacy is manifested when team members dismiss an argument or position by demanding concrete facts from specific sources and rejecting anything that lacks immediate substantiation. They fundamentally argue that unless something can be proven beyond doubt at that moment, it must be false. This fallacy is often paired with other fallacies (emotion, authority) to stop discussions from happening.

This fallacy is problematic for several reasons:

1. **Burden of Proof Shift:** Appeal to Ignorance shifts this burden unfairly onto the opposing side.

    * This demands that the person with the new idea or argument prove it conclusively, even though the burden of proof should be shared.

1. **Stifling Innovation and Exploration:** Demanding immediate concrete facts can stifle innovative thinking and the exploration of new ideas.

    * Progress often depends on considering and discussing concepts that may not have been fully fleshed out or backed by solid evidence yet.

1. **Complexity of Proof:** Ideas, related to complex topics or future trends, cannot be proven or disproven with immediate concrete facts.

    * Requiring such proof disregards the nuances and intricacies of certain subjects.

1. **Erosion of Trust:** Demanding immediate proof can erode trust within a team.

    * It creates an atmosphere of skepticism and suspicion, making team members hesitant to share novel ideas for fear of being dismissed out of hand.

1. **Slowing Progress:** The insistence on immediate proof leads to analysis paralysis.

    * Valuable time and energy are wasted in trying to provide foolproof evidence rather than focusing on productive discussions and collaborations.

1. **Missed Opportunities:** Breakthroughs and insights emerge from speculative or preliminary discussions.

    * Dismissing ideas too early due to lack of immediate proof can lead to missed opportunities.

To foster a productive and innovative team environment, it's important to approach discussions in good faith and with an understanding, that not all ideas need to be fully proven at the outset. Instead of defaulting to an **Appeal to Ignorance**, team members should engage in open-minded and respectful exchanges, allowing ideas to be presented, refined, and critiqued over time. This approach encourages the exploration of new concepts, maintains trust among team members, and allows for more comprehensive and forward-thinking discussions.
