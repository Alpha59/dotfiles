# Topic Evaluation and Ranking Criteria

### Background

As part of DS2 LS, we would like to maintain a list of topics to be presented in the next 3-6 months. This gives time for the presenter to create impactful presentations without creating churn for them. As part of this exercise, its important to identify the right set of topics to be presented. The purpose of this document is to present evaluation and ranking criteria for topics.

### **Criteria to gather topics that cover depth and breath of DS2
**

1. Topics are sourced based on audience interest and needs, reflecting current trends and challenges relevant to DS2 members. This in turn increases participation from DS2 members since they are more likely to attend a topic they voted on.
2. It is encouraged to share complex problems and their solutions across DS2 to increase visibility on the challenges and to foster learning from other groups.
3. Topics are diverse to include perspectives relevant to different functional groups within DS2 (SDEs, SDMs, TPMs, QAEs).
4. ~~The content is engaging and presented in a manner that fosters learning across different job roles.~~
5. ~~Promotes cross-functional understanding and encourages collaboration.~~

### Process to gather topics from DS2 members

We will be conducting a **survey at the beginning and end of each session** to gather feedback on potential future topics.

The survey should be shared with attendees in the chat at the start of each session, with a brief explanation of its purpose and a request for them to participate. Here is a suggested chat blurb:

>"Hello everyone! We want your input on future topics for our learning series. Please take a few minutes to complete this short survey to vote on the topics you would find most valuable: [insert survey link] Your feedback will help us ensure the series continues to meet your learning needs. Thank you for your participation!"


At the end of the session, please remind attendees to complete the survey if they haven't already done so. You can use a similar message:

>"Just a quick reminder to please take a moment before you go to complete the survey on future learning topics. Your input is greatly appreciated and will help shape the direction of our series. The survey link is available in the chat."


Rationale:
Sharing the survey at the beginning of the session provides ample time for attendees to respond, as they may need to pause and thoughtfully consider the options. Reminding them again at the end helps capture feedback from those who may have been engaged in the session content and forgot to complete the survey earlier.


### Ranking criteria for topics

**Weighted Topic Score = (Organizational Alignment x 0.3) + (Demand/Need x 0.3) + (Pipeline and many AWS accountsl x 0.4)**
**Where:**

Organizational Alignment (0-10 scale):

    * This represents the level of alignment between the topic and the organization's strategic goals, values, and competency framework.
    * A higher score indicates a stronger alignment.
    * Organizational Alignment = Strategic Goal Alignment (0-4 points) + Core Value Alignment (0-3 points) + Competency Alignment (0-3 points)

Demand/Need (0-10 scale):

    * This reflects the data gathered by our surveys regarding favorable Topics.
    * A higher score indicates a greater need or demand for the topic.

Impact Potential (0-10 scale):

    * This represents the potential for the topic to improve individual and organizational performance, as well as its contribution to the overall development of employees.
    * A higher score indicates a greater potential for positive impact.
    * Impact Potential Score = Individual Impact (0-4 points) + Organizational Impact (0-4 points) + Scalability and Sustainability (0-2 points)

The weightage factors (0.3, 0.3, and 0.4) are used to prioritize the different criteria, with the "Impact Potential" being the most heavily weighted factor, as it captures the overall value and benefit of the topic.

Here's an example application of the formula:
Topic A: “Unlocking GraphQL: Learn how we use it in Amazon to revolutionize API interactions”:

* Organizational Alignment: 4
* Demand/Need: 6
* Impact Potential: 9


Weighted Topic Score = (4 x 0.3) + (6 x 0.3) + (9 x 0.4) = 6.6

Topic B: “Code Perfection: Mastering the Craft of Clean Code”:

* Organizational Alignment: 6
* Demand/Need: 8
* Impact Potential: 7

 Weighted Topic Score = (6 x 0.3) + (8 x 0.3) + (7 x 0.4) = 7.0

In this example, Topic B has a higher Weighted Topic Score (7.0) compared to Topic A (6.6), indicating that Topic B should be prioritized for inclusion in the learning series.
