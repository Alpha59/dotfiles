Welcome to the Amazon Device Reverse Logistics Team! We believe in the power of mechanisms, ceremonies, and shared philosophies to drive our success. # Roles

This document serves as a comprehensive guide to the roles within our software team, providing clear descriptions, responsibilities, and rotation schedules for each key position. These roles are integral to the smooth functioning of our team and the successful execution of our projects. By understanding the distinct responsibilities associated with each role, we aim to foster a culture of clarity, collaboration, and accountability within our team. Whether you are a seasoned team member or new to our organization, this resource will serve as a valuable reference, ensuring that everyone is aligned with their specific roles and responsibilities, ultimately contributing to the achievement of our team's objectives.

## Single-Threaded Owner / Single Point of Contact

**Description:** The Single-Threaded Owner is a pivotal role within the team responsible for owning and driving a specific initiative, task, program, project, deliverable, or function. This role is unique because it operates with a singular focus, dedicating its attention and efforts to the success of the assigned initiative.

**Responsibilities:**

1. **Ownership and Leadership:** Assume full ownership of the designated initiative, taking leadership and responsibility for its success.
2. **Progress and Momentum:** Drive the initiative forward, making continuous progress, and maintaining momentum.
3. **Strategic Planning:** Develop and execute strategic plans, designs, and roadmaps for the initiative, setting clear goals and milestones.
    1. Designs are always done with the team’s input, but the Single Threaded Owner is responsible for the creation of the finalized Design Document.
4. **Cross-Functional Collaboration:** Collaborate with cross-functional teams or manage a team dedicated to the initiative, ensuring cohesive efforts.
    1. Single Threaded owners should be present in all meetings about the initiative. They may not lead the meeting, but maintaining knowledge of all parts of the project and decisions is critical to the role.
5. **Problem-Solving:** Address challenges and obstacles that may arise during the initiative's execution, finding innovative solutions.
6. **Communication:** Keep stakeholders informed about the progress, challenges, and achievements of the initiative.
7. **Accountability:** Be accountable for the initiative's outcomes and results, striving for excellence.


**Rotation:** Assigned on a per-project level

## **CR On-Call**

**Description:** The CR On-Call role is a crucial position responsible for ensuring the smooth flow of code changes within the team. This role requires the team member to be readily available to address and resolve issues related to code reviews (CR's). The CR On-Call serves as the initial point of contact for CR reviews, acting swiftly to review and respond to Code Reviews.

When you are the assigned CR On-Call, you should expect to spend about an hour of your day addressing the below priorities.

**Responsibilities:**

1. **CR Review:** Be on standby to promptly review and assess any code review (CR) requests submitted by team members, ensuring that code changes meet quality and compliance standards.
2. **Communication:** Effectively communicate any open CR's or critical issues to the team, providing relevant information to facilitate quick resolution.


**Rotation:** [Rotated Daily](https://oncall.corp.amazon.com/#/view/kindle-rl-dev-cr/schedule)

## **Primary On-Call**

**Description:** The Primary On-Call role is a critical position within the team, responsible for addressing and resolving real-time operational issues and ensuring the reliability of systems and services 24x7. This role involves handling all incoming RL Tech (Reverse Logistics Technology) issues, coordinating solutions, and continuously improving the team's operational excellence.

**Responsibilities:**

1. **24x7 Availability:** Serve as the primary on-call team member, available around the clock to respond to operational issues and incidents.
2. **Issue Resolution:** Take ownership of all incoming RL Tech issues, prioritize them based on severity, and work diligently to troubleshoot, identify root causes, fix, and thoroughly test solutions. Ensure that all issues are resolved promptly and in accordance with Service-Level Agreements (SLAs).
3. **Ticket Policy Improvement:** Continuously assess and enhance the team's ticket policy, eliminating trivial or unnecessary alarming, and making necessary adjustments to the codebase to streamline operations.
4. **Documentation:** Contribute to the documentation effort by creating and updating the on-call documentation in the team's wiki, providing clear and concise instructions for common issue fixes and resolutions.
5. **Operational Excellence:** Collaborate with the team to improve Operational Excellence metrics, enhance visibility into operational processes, and refine operational procedures for increased efficiency and effectiveness.
6. **SAS Risk Resolution:** Address SAS (Software Assurance Services) risks by allocating one point for fixing the highest-count VersionSets first. Prioritize and expedite deployment in the pipeline to mitigate potential risks.


**Rotation:** [Rotated Weekly](https://oncall.corp.amazon.com/#/view/kindle-rl-dev-primary/schedule)

## **Secondary On-Call**

**Description:** The Secondary On-Call role is a crucial support position within the team, primarily focused on assisting the Primary On-Call team member during sev-2 (severity 2) incidents and providing additional coverage for operational tasks. This role ensures that there is continuous coverage and support for critical incidents, as well as helps manage ticket overflow and address low-severity inquiries from customers and stakeholders.

**Responsibilities:**

1. **Sev-2 Assistance:** Remain available and responsive when the Primary On-Call team member requires assistance in handling sev-2 (Severity 2) incidents. Collaborate closely to help troubleshoot and resolve critical issues promptly.
2. **Ticket Overflow:** Take responsibility for handling ticket overflow that exceeds the capacity of the Primary On-Call team member. Ensure that all incoming tickets are addressed, prioritized, and resolved effectively.
3. **Customer/Stakeholder Inquiries:** Field and respond to low-severity questions, inquiries, or requests from customers and stakeholders. Provide clear and helpful information, or escalate as needed.
4. **SAS Campaigns:** View and attempt to resolve SAS (Software Assurance Services) Campaigns, contributing to the team's efforts to address potential risks and ensure software quality.

## Sprint Facilitator

**Description:** The Sprint Facilitator is a critical role within the team, responsible for overseeing and facilitating various meetings throughout the sprint cycle. This role was introduced as a proactive measure to prevent burnout among team members by distributing the responsibilities associated with running meetings. The Sprint Facilitator acts as a de facto Scrum Master, ensuring that meetings are well-organized, productive, and supportive of the team's objectives. This role has the freedom to introduce innovative elements into meetings to enhance their effectiveness and engagement.

**Responsibilities:**

1. **Daily Stand-up Meetings:** Lead and facilitate the daily stand-up meetings, ensuring that they remain focused, efficient, and within the allotted time frame. The Sprint Facilitator has the authority to choose a "Question of the Day" to enhance team engagement.
2. **Sprint Meetings:** Organize and conduct various sprint meetings, including Retrospectives, Open Hours, Sprint Planning, and others. Keep the team on the agenda, maintain focus, and guide discussions to productive outcomes.
3. **Meeting Facilitation:** Act as the facilitator during meetings, ensuring that they run smoothly, discussions remain on topic, and conflicts are resolved effectively. Keep meetings moving forward and encourage open and constructive communication.
4. **Sprint Board Administration:** Administer and maintain the sprint board, ensuring that it accurately reflects the current status of the sprint. This involves updating and organizing tasks, ensuring clarity, and tracking progress.
5. **Unblocking Team Members:** Investigate and address issues that may be blocking team members' progress during the sprint. Collaborate with others to find solutions and ensure the team's overall productivity.
6. **Respect Retro Items:** Ensure that retro items from the previous sprint are respected and followed up on. Facilitate discussions on how to implement improvements based on retro feedback.
7. **Pointing Meetings:** Schedule pointing meetings before sprint planning if necessary, ensuring that stories are appropriately estimated for the upcoming sprint.
8. **Open Hours Management:** Check the Open Hours schedule on designated days (e.g., Monday and Wednesday) to determine if any topics are available for discussion. If no topics are suggested, the Sprint Facilitator should consider canceling Open Hours for that day.
9. **Introduce Personal Style:** Bring a personal style and innovative ideas to meetings, making them engaging, effective, and tailored to the team's needs.


More information on Scrum Masters can be found here: https://www.atlassian.com/agile/scrum/scrum-master

## The Power of Mechanisms

At the heart of our team's success lies the recognition that good intentions alone won't suffice; we need effective mechanisms. Mechanisms are complete processes that transform inputs into desired outputs on a recurring basis. They enable us to learn from historical data, scale process-based solutions, and prevent the loss of institutional knowledge.

### Avoiding Exhaustion

Exhaustion is mankind's default mechanism, but we aim for something better. Working harder or doubling down won't solve long-term problems. We use mechanisms to strike a balance, deciding when to pull resources appropriately.

Avoiding exhaustion aligns with Ownership. We think long term and prioritize the well-being of our team members, ensuring their sustained contributions. We act on behalf of the entire team and the company, recognizing that a sustainable team is essential for long-term success.

* Promote responsible resource allocation for the team's long-term health.
* Prevent overexertion and burnout among team members.
* Ensure a balanced approach to tackling challenges.
* Foster a culture of Ownership where everyone cares for the team's well-being.

The team has suffered from exhaustion caused by any number of factors. These mechanisms and strategies are designed to defeat these traps that have plagued the team.


>***Good intentions never work, you need good mechanisms to make anything happen***.


There are some tasks that can be completed by requesting additional resources by moved towards the task. These are **Good Intentions** where the tech and business goals are being aligned.

### Retrospect: The Starting Point

Retrospect serves as the foundation for Customer Obsession. It provides a feedback loop to ensure we continuously improve, aligning our processes with customer needs and expectations. We obsess over customer satisfaction and trust. Retrospect helps us understand and meet their ever-evolving needs effectively.

### Effective Resource Allocation

Raising priorities and creating a sense of urgency can be counterproductive. Good mechanisms help us discern when it's correct to allocate resources and when it's not. Adding more resources isn't a guarantee of success; we focus on smart allocation.

Effective resource allocation is rooted in Think Big. We aim to align our resource investments with bold directions that inspire results and innovation. We think differently and investigate how to serve customers better by allocating resources strategically rather than impulsively.

* Evaluate resource allocation requests objectively in alignment with our bold directions.
* Prioritize tasks based on their strategic importance.
* Balance resource allocation to maintain overall productivity.
* Avoid resource drain on critical areas while thinking big.

>Long-term, you can’t solve problems by working more or trying harder


In the long term, those resources are now being taken from somewhere else. Those resources are removed from on-call, or a project, or a different request. This does not imply that high severity issues should never pull resources. **Good Mechanisms** can create a balance between when it is appropriate to pull resources, and when it is not.

### Collaboration and Quality

Collaboration and maintaining quality align with Insisting on the Highest Standards. We relentlessly pursue high standards to deliver quality products and services to our customers. We drive our teams to deliver high-quality results, ensuring that defects do not get sent down the line and that problems are fixed, so they stay fixed.

* Foster effective collaboration and communication among team members.
* Encourage team members to support each other, just as we seek diverse perspectives.
* Maintain a high standard of quality in our work, minimizing errors.
* Ensure that defects are addressed promptly and fixed permanently.

>Doubling down, trying harder, or creating a sense of urgency won't make the 1812 Overture shorter than 15 minutes, and it won't get software problems solved faster.


Issues in the software are not resolved by simply dedicating more resources or making them higher priority. Things that require design, implementation, or even just triage, often have a high unknown in how long they will take. Trying to add additional resources can help alleviate stress, but does not necessarily halve the implementation time. Just as it takes 1 violinist 15 minutes to play their part of the 1812 Overture, 1000 violinists will take 15 minutes as well. Adding resources is not a bad thing, though. When a single violinist makes a mistake, it is easy to hear, and may have long-term consequences. When a single violinist is off tempo, there is no supporting orchestra to return them to tempo, which will extend the length of the overture. 1000 violinists (or even just 10) can watch out for each other, cover mistakes, and average out to a tempo. This prevents the audience (or customer) from ever knowing that mistakes were made, and ensuring that the overture is played at the expected or predicted time.

### Mechanisms for Success

Our mechanisms do more than keep us on track; they prevent blockers, promote knowledge sharing, and enhance team member satisfaction. Mechanisms are an ongoing commitment, not one-time solutions.

Our mechanisms are rooted in the tenet of earning trust. We listen attentively, speak candidly, treat others respectfully, and benchmark ourselves against the best to build trust within our team and with our stakeholders. We aim to earn trust through reliability, transparency, and accountability in our mechanisms.

* Prevent and address blockers in our processes, demonstrating reliability.
* Promote knowledge sharing and institutional learning to build trust within the team.
* Enhance team member satisfaction and motivation, contributing to a positive work environment.
* Maintain a resilient and adaptable team culture that fosters trust.

Where the goal is to finish quickly, or throw more resources at a problem, then a mechanism needs to exist. Without a mechanism, team members are quickly worn down. In a marathon, a runner may utilize the default mechanism of exhaustion. This will likely result in running a few miles at top speed (if they are well-trained and well motivated), and then collapsing before crossing the finish line. A seasoned runner takes this into account, and paces themselves based on practice and experience. They choose a goal time, and stick to it. At an 8-minute pace, they will try to maintain the same pace throughout the race. They will have scheduled water breaks, as to not succumb to dehydration. Exhaustion only becomes a mechanism in the final stretch, where they know that pushing their boundaries is acceptable.

A running team will utilize a mechanism involving a pacer. This runner will rotate through the group, and sets the pace that the group moves at. The pacer is then instructed not to fall behind, or increase the pace during their rotation - since that may lead to unnecessary exhaustion. Although one runner in the group may be faster, and another slower, the pacer mechanism allows all members to move along at the same pace. This will lead to an eventual victory for the team. The faster and slower runners are given their opportunity to differentiate themselves from the team at the end of the marathon, where they can push their limits in a controlled setting.

Mechanisms, however, are not designed purely to keep people on task, or on pace. They can also be used to prevent blockers, prevent institutional knowledge, and improve team member satisfaction. It is only by looking at the mistakes that at team has made that one can confidently create a mechanism to prevent that mistake in the future. Mechanisms need to be continual. A one-time mechanism is never enough, as the process being avoided will just snowball into the same issue again. A mechanism needs to be on a regular interval (or triggered on a regular interval) to have the desired effect.

Every team member starts with a single mechanism: Retrospect. This ongoing feedback loop fosters continuous improvement. From here, all other mechanisms stem, ensuring we adapt and evolve. Retrospect is the cornerstone of our team's success. It introduces early and frequent feedback, crucial for our progress. By using Retrospect, we observe what mechanisms work and which ones need improvement.

**Purpose**: Retrospect serves as a reflection of our commitment to Learn and Be Curious. We are never done learning and always seek to improve ourselves and our processes through continuous feedback. We explore new possibilities and act on them by continuously adapting and refining our mechanisms.

* Facilitate open discussions on our team's performance to promote learning.
* Identify areas for improvement and growth, fostering curiosity.
* Celebrate achievements and successes as part of our learning process.
* Ensure that all other mechanisms are evaluated and optimized over time.

>Everyone should start with just a single mechanism: Retrospect. A frequent feedback loop allows for continuous improvement. From that single mechanism, all other mechanisms will grow.

In an idealized world, always completing all sprint tasks (and never needing to bump or pull forward a task) would result in 100% project completion. This is of course unreasonable to expect. ## Retrospective

**Purpose:** This procedure aims to ensure that the insights and action items generated during Retrospectives are not forgotten, but are instead effectively captured and implemented for continuous improvement.

**Steps:**

1. **Conduct Retrospectives:** Regularly schedule and conduct Retrospective meetings as part of the team's standard workflow.
2. **Record Insights:** During the Retrospective meeting, the Sprint Facilitator should take detailed notes or minutes of the discussions. Ensure that all identified insights, challenges, and action items are documented.
3. **Action Item Assignments:** Assign responsible team members for each action item identified during the Retrospective. Clearly define roles and responsibilities for implementation.
4. **Documentation:** Store the Retrospective minutes or notes in a centralized and accessible location, such as a shared drive or collaboration platform. Ensure that team members can easily reference them.
5. **Implementation Tracking:** Track the progress of action items and ensure they are implemented within the agreed-upon timeframe.
    1. This can be done by pulling specific retro items into the sprint


**Responsible Party:** Sprint Facilitator

**Frequency:** After each Retrospective meeting and as needed for action item follow-up.

## Stand-Up Meetings and Communication

**Purpose:** The Stand-Up Meetings and Communication procedure defines the process for conducting stand-up meetings and leveraging communication channels to share updates, address ambiguity, and seek help when needed. Effective communication in stand-up meetings enhances collaboration and knowledge sharing within the team.

**Steps:**

1. **Scheduled Stand-Up Meetings:** Schedule regular stand-up meetings, ideally held at the same time each day, to facilitate team communication.
2. **Communication Channel:** Utilize a [designated Slack channel for stand-up updates](https://amzn-devices.slack.com/archives/C030JNC9SPN). Before and after the meeting, team members can share updates, engage in discussions, and request help by creating threads within the channel.
    1. Start with "Standup update for:"
    2. Mention the team member's alias (e.g., @<alias>)
    3. Include the date
    4. Provide updates on the following:
        1. "Yesterday: What did you accomplish yesterday for our milestones?"
        2. "Today: What do you plan to accomplish today for our milestones?"
        3. "Help Requested from: @<alias>" (if assistance is needed)
    5. A Stand-up Workflow is available in the standup channel to enforce formatting
3. **Thread Format:** When posting questions related to stand-up updates in the Slack channel, create a thread for the specific stand-up update.
4. **Ambiguity Resolution:** Use stand-up meetings as an opportunity to share any ambiguity or challenges faced during tasks. Encourage team members to seek help from colleagues who may have more knowledge or expertise in the area.
5. **Active Participation:** Encourage active participation from all team members during stand-up meetings. Ensure that each team member has an opportunity to share their updates and seek help if necessary.
6. **Documentation:** Maintain records of stand-up updates and discussions in the designated Slack channel. This serves as a reference for team members and provides visibility into ongoing work.

**Responsible Party:** Sprint Facilitator

**Frequency:** Stand-up meetings should be held daily

Effective stand-up meetings and communication channels promote transparency, knowledge sharing, and timely issue resolution within the team.

## On-Call Responsibilities and Daily SOP

**Purpose:** The On-Call Responsibilities and Daily Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) outlines the tasks and responsibilities of an on-call team member. It provides a structured approach to managing the on-call queue, addressing various ticket types, and improving team operations.

**Steps:**


1. **Organize On-Call Queue:**
    1. Start by organizing the on-call queue, prioritizing the oldest tickets. Begin scanning from the bottom of the queue and work upward.
    2. Identify and close out "quick wins" – tickets that can be resolved in less than 10 minutes. This includes tasks such as canceling WSR (Workstation Request) requests, closing customer tickets that have been inactive while waiting for a reply, responding to simple information requests, or closing bulk tickets related to common outages.
    3. Address any tickets that should have a quick resolution but are uncertain how to resolve them promptly. Collaborate with a teammate for assistance, promoting knowledge sharing.
2. **Address High-Priority Tickets:**
    1. Review the queue for high-priority tickets that require immediate attention. This may include large impacting bugs in the FC (Fulfillment Center), DLQ (Dead Letter Queue) tickets open for 7+ days, customer tickets idle on the team's end for 3+ days, and herd tickets.
    2. Explore systematic solutions to eliminate these issues, rather than addressing each specific ticket individually.
3. **Prevent Recurrence:**
    1. Deep dive into tickets that need preventive measures to avoid future occurrences. Focus on improving team operations and preventing similar issues from arising in the future.
    2. Root cause analysis should be conducted to ensure that resolved issues do not reoccur.
4. **Check External Requests Folder:**
    1. Examine the Device Reverse Logistics Tech - External Requests folder and check for any tickets that should be in the On-Call folder. Move them to the appropriate location and repeat Step 1.
5. **Proactive Improvements:**
    1. If there are no tickets to work on while on-call, proactively look for ways to enhance the team's operations. This may involve:
    2. Unblocking pipelines to ensure smooth workflow.
    3. Resolving Software Assurance Service (SAS) risks to maintain service quality.
    4. Identifying and removing dead code.
    5. Writing functional tests to improve code reliability.
    6. Participating in code refactoring to enhance codebase quality.

**Responsible Party:** On-Call Team Member

**Frequency:** The On-Call Responsibilities and Daily SOP should be followed during each on-call shift to efficiently manage the on-call queue and contribute to continuous improvement.

Following this procedure ensures that on-call team members effectively manage the queue, address priority issues, and actively contribute to improving team operations and service quality.

## Stories

In agile development there are several different types of tickets or stories which can be defined. The most prominent of which is User Stories. User stories are descriptions of work from the customer perspective. This is a direct correlation to the “Customer Obsession” leadership principle.


>Leaders start with the customer and work backwards. They work vigorously to earn and keep customer trust. Although leaders pay attention to competitors, they obsess over customers. [Learn More](https://w.amazon.com/bin/view/LeadershipPrinciples/CustomerObsession)


For this reason, all stories start with the same syntax in the summary:


>As a [customer type] I want ... So that...


The customer type can be defined in numerous ways – but should be specific to a type of customer. Examples include (but are not limited to) FC Associates, CS Associates, Developers, Lab126 Users, or Team Leadership (Kevin/Jason). Customers may want various things, but we must remember these are Wants, and not needs. Any customer request can be pushed back on, as long as the pushback is justified.

What drives customer wants may vary, so we must explain “So that” in a way that makes it as difficult as possible to push back against customers. For an FC Associate, their wants may be driven by an operational change - or a desire to simplify a process, and for team leadership, wants may be derived from organizational changes, or initiatives. The goal of the summary is to help developers (and business users who are prioritizing work) to “Realize the Reward” of this story.

### Purpose

Maintaining the leadership principles, creating good stories, and following standard procedures helps us to “Earn Trust” not only with our customers, but also with our teammates.


>Leaders listen attentively, speak candidly, and treat others respectfully. They are vocally self-critical, even when doing so is awkward or embarrassing. Leaders do not believe their or their team’s body odor smells of perfume. They benchmark themselves and their teams against the best. [Learn More](https://w.amazon.com/bin/view/LeadershipPrinciples/EarnTrust)


For this purpose, we need to provide as much detail as we can in our tickets, mentioning any possible call outs and spreading information to our team members as effectively as possible. Although not all stories will fill all of the same requirements and meet all of the same criteria, adding as much detail as possible to try to convey the information as effectively as possible will never hurt.

Especially on teams where we are permitted to work from home, or communication can be compromised by schedule, information bias, or the [Framing Effect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/medicine-and-dentistry/framing-bias) stories should stand on their own. Although discretion can be used to ensure that time is not wasted adding excessive detail, a reasonable assessment should be made that any new hire should be able to complete any story without help.

## Pipeline Unblocking Tasks

**Purpose:** The Pipeline Unblocking Tasks procedure establishes a structured approach to address pipeline blockages by defining specific tasks and actions required to resolve issues. This ensures that pipeline issues are actively and effectively tackled.

**Steps:**

2. **Documentation:** Document the details of the pipeline blockage, including any error messages, logs, or relevant information in a ticket.

5. **Communication:** Notify relevant stakeholders about the pipeline blockage and the action plan for resolution. Keep stakeholders informed about progress and any delays.
6. **Documentation Update:** Update documentation related to the pipeline, configurations, or processes as needed to prevent similar blockages in the future.
7. **Preventive Measures:** Implement preventive measures or automated checks to proactively detect and address potential pipeline blockages in the future.

**Responsible Party:** Team Member Assigned to Resolve Pipeline Blockage

**Frequency:** This procedure should be followed whenever pipeline blockages are identified, and tasks are needed for resolution.

The procedure aims to ensure that pipeline blockages are addressed promptly and systematically by defining specific tasks and actions required for resolution.

## Tracking Unplanned Work

**Purpose:** The Tracking Unplanned Work procedure establishes a method for the team to document and manage any unplanned work or high-priority tasks that arise during the sprint, ensuring visibility and accountability for such work items.

**Steps:**

**Responsible Party:** Team members are responsible for adding their own unplanned work

## Clarification Process for Design Documents

**Purpose:** The Clarification Process for Design Documents procedure provides a structured approach for team members to seek clarifications, request updates, and add additional information to design documents. 4. **Contextual Comments:** When adding clarification questions, provide context by referencing the specific sections or content in the document that you are inquiring about.

7. **Document Updates:** If the document owner makes updates or provides clarifications in response to your questions, review the document to ensure that your queries have been addressed.
8. **Escalation:** If your clarification questions or requests for additional information are not addressed in a timely manner, follow established escalation procedures to ensure timely resolution.

**Responsible Party:** All Team Members

**Frequency:** This procedure should be followed whenever team members have questions or require additional information while reviewing design documents.

The procedure aims to enhance communication and collaboration related to design documents by promoting a structured approach to seeking clarifications and providing additional context.
