## Summary
**As a** team lead or manager, **I want** to create or complete a **team mechanisms document**, **so that** the team has a comprehensive, well-defined set of processes and guidelines to follow for consistent and efficient operations, decision-making, and collaboration.

A well-documented set of mechanisms helps teams maintain clarity in roles, responsibilities, workflows, and escalations. It also standardizes procedures and tools, fostering a structured environment that improves performance, reduces errors, and ensures all team members are aligned with key goals and processes.

### Vision
By creating or completing the team mechanisms document, the team will have a clear guide outlining standard operating procedures (SOPs), communication protocols, review processes, and escalation paths. This document ensures alignment on operational expectations and provides consistency across projects and team tasks.

### Background
Team mechanisms refer to the documented processes that govern how a team operates, including development workflows, communication norms, escalation procedures, and decision-making protocols. These mechanisms are crucial for scaling teams, ensuring smooth operations, and reducing ambiguity during daily work.

### Purpose
The purpose of this story is to create or complete the **team mechanisms document** to formalize the team’s operations, making it easy for new and existing team members to understand the processes and workflows. This document will also provide a consistent reference for handling various team scenarios, such as deployments, incident management, and code reviews.

## Details

### 1. **Core Mechanisms to Include**
   - **Development Process**: Define how the team handles the entire development lifecycle, from feature requests and code reviews to testing and deployments.
     - Tools: Git workflows, pull request guidelines, code review best practices.
     - Documentation: Requirements for code comments, design documents, and test cases.

   - **Incident Management**: Outline the process for handling incidents, including severity levels, on-call rotations, and response timelines.
     - Tools: Incident tracking (PagerDuty, OpsGenie), response playbooks, post-mortem procedures.
     - Escalation: Define the escalation paths for Sev1, Sev2, etc.

   - **Communication Protocols**: Define the communication methods and frequency for the team’s standups, sprint planning, retrospectives, and team meetings.
     - Tools: Slack, email, or project management software (e.g., Jira, Trello).
     - Roles: Define key team roles and expectations for communication.

   - **Decision-Making and Escalation**: Document decision-making protocols for key technical and operational decisions, including ownership and escalation policies when disagreements arise.
     - RACI Matrix: Clearly assign responsibilities for tasks and decisions (Responsible, Accountable, Consulted, Informed).

   - **Review Mechanisms**: Set up recurring reviews for code quality, operational health, and project status.
     - Metrics: Define how the team tracks success (e.g., code quality metrics, operational health, velocity).

   - **Onboarding/Offboarding**: Define the process for onboarding new team members, including training, access setup, and role definitions. Likewise, provide clear steps for offboarding.

### 2. **Draft or Review Existing Content**
   - Collect existing documentation, mechanisms, or guidelines the team may already have.
   - Fill in any gaps by gathering input from team members and stakeholders on processes that need to be better defined or updated.
   - Ensure all relevant sections are covered, including roles, responsibilities, tools, and workflows.

### 3. **Collaboration and Feedback**
   - Engage the team to review the draft document and provide feedback. Ensure it reflects actual practices and allows for flexibility where necessary.
   - Use collaborative tools like Google Docs or Confluence to gather and incorporate feedback from team members.

### 4. **Version Control and Accessibility**
   - Ensure the document is easily accessible to all team members (e.g., in a shared workspace like Confluence, SharePoint, or an internal wiki).
   - Set up a version control system for the document so that updates can be tracked over time, with an assigned owner for maintaining it.

### 5. **Iterative Improvements**
   - Schedule periodic reviews (e.g., quarterly) to update the team mechanisms document to reflect changes in team structure, technology, or processes.
   - Encourage continuous feedback from team members to identify areas where the mechanisms could be improved.

### Testing
- Conduct a "dry run" using the mechanisms in real-world scenarios (e.g., follow the incident management process during a Sev2 issue).
- Gather feedback on whether the documented procedures align with actual workflows and adjust where necessary.

## Acceptance Criteria
- Should create or complete the team mechanisms document, covering key areas such as development processes, communication protocols, and incident management.
- Should involve the team in reviewing the document to ensure it reflects real-world practices.
- Should ensure the document is version-controlled and accessible to all team members.
- Should schedule periodic reviews to keep the document up-to-date and relevant.
